### Task progress, research judgment and inferred acceleration

[METR's November 20 report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/) evaluates public models through Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, rather than every model released in November. Read main methods/results/robustness/discussion, Appendix A on baselines, Appendix H on growth and Appendix M on task creation. Individual test dates are not uniformly reported; some human comparisons end in August.

- **Time horizons:** 222 TH1.1 tasks and 36 TH2 tasks in 12 families, typically 15 models using a ReAct scaffold with terminal/Python/image tools and task-specific budgets. Only 34 TH2 tasks have human-time points: 31 estimates and three successful formal baselines. Human baselines permit Sonnet 5 for implementation, not high-level research decisions. The combined 50%-success human-duration scale doubles in 3.61 months (95% interval 2.08–4.94); verifiable TH2 alone gives a different trend. These are not autonomous runtimes or completion rates across all jobs. Detected reward hacks receive zero; approximately 36 refused/blocked observations are excluded. Task composition, estimated difficulty and changed human assistance limit comparisons with earlier horizons.
- **Research judgment:** agents create new METR tasks with 48 hours, 100M tokens, three concurrent test environments and internet access; people set goals, examples and rubric. They can test against frontier/lagging agents with separate token pools and a final test budget. None of the generated tasks was useful enough to enter this report's suite. Model judges overrate tasks generated by models equally strong or stronger than themselves relative to human ratings. The direction of this comparison matters. Additional guidance did not materially fix the gap. Unused resources and underelicitation remain plausible, and strong verifiable ML performance prevents generalizing to inability to do all research.
- **Growth inference:** combined public-frontier MCI shows no clear acceleration/deceleration. Developer-specific slopes depend on calibration models, breakpoint and task/index choices. Appendix H's comparable public Anthropic subset is distinct from the November 5 report's private evidence; the two are not direct replications of the same dataset. Public release timing and the unobserved AI-free counterfactual prevent attributing or ruling out AI-caused acceleration. The report does not establish absence of self-improvement or rule out future rapid acceleration.
