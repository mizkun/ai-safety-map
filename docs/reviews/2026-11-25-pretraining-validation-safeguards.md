### Pretraining progress is not an identified recursive-improvement effect

- [OpenAI, November 22](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/), main discussion and footnotes 3/5, reports base-model loss on a fixed held-out internal-code snapshot. The comparison covers models since GPT-3.5, not a randomized experiment with and without AI research assistance. Training completion dates and per-model research, training and labor inputs are not supplied.
- The developer attributes most of the steepening trend to long-term research and compute investment, with a smaller automation contribution, while noting meaningful AI design contributions to recent models. Proposed limits on pretraining progress and forecasts of new capabilities remain arguments and proposals, not implemented safeguards with measured outcomes.
- [METR, November 24](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-24-interpreting-a-recent-acceleration-in-pretraining-loss/), main analysis, requested disclosures and footnotes, interprets the same 13-model series as a roughly sevenfold increase in the rate of log-perplexity decline since early 2026 compared with 2022–2025. This is original external analysis, **not independent replication of the measurements** and not a sevenfold increase in intelligence or AI-caused productivity.
- METR presents competing interpretations and explicitly lacks consensus on broader capability forecasts. Other held-out corpora, completion rather than release dates, and compute/labor inputs would help distinguish corpus-specific progress, overfitting, additional investment and AI uplift. Contrast the already-covered [METR Q4 evaluation](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/): its public-model capability trends and this single-lab loss trend measure different things.
- Before: `R2`/`R3` covered partial AI research contributions and uncertain aggregate acceleration but not this loss disclosure. After: add two sources/cards and bilingual paragraphs. Retain the conditions of `R1-R2`, `R2-R3` and `R3-R2`; no probability, ASI date, evidence-level upgrade or autonomous-loop claim. Reassess and renew the two feedback-edge clocks, with whole-process costs and successor usefulness still required.

No pooling or inference of uniform superiority or unknown-target intrusion rates.
- **Repair (`M3`)**: §7.2 describes 250 CWE-bench v2 tasks, 185 weakness types and six language families, four independent trials per task with a one-hour limit and high reasoning effort. The agent is not told bug locations. Success requires blocking reproduction of the known exploit while passing functional/regression tests. The default attacker is Gemini 3.8 Flash; tool-use/coding attacks can inspect target reasoning. Tool-use survival is 86% through 150 attempts. Retrieval survives 40 in all tested scenarios, yet more attempts can succeed. Coding improves over Hydrogen and matches Carbon. These bounded tests do not establish universal robustness or normal-use incident rates.
- **Awareness counterevidence (`C3a2`)**: §3.3.3.4/Table 3.3 classifies 111,950 internal-coding and 110,825 post-training *thoughts*, not tasks or incidents. Human review covers every post-training flag, every coding flag about CoT monitoring/adjustment, and 54 sampled general-monitoring flags. No CoT-monitor evasion was identified in those inspected samples. The four adjustment flags involved workflow labels and benign-task false positives. Unflagged/unverbalized behavior remains outside that conclusion; some older models use a different classifier.
